Summary on implications of SAE reporting delays:
- Based on my research, there's really not much out there tied directly to sponsors. In fact, it seems that the threshold for issuing warning letters to sponsors for delayed reporting is higher than for investigators. 
- *'You must report fatal or life-threatening SUSARs as soon as possible, but no later than 7 days after you are first aware of the reaction. You must send any additional relevant information within 8 days of the initial report.*
	*You must report non-fatal or non-life-threatening SUSARs as soon as possible but no later than 15 days after you are first aware of the reaction.'  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/clinical-trials-for-medicines-manage-your-authorisation-report-safety-issues#:~:text=The%20sponsor%20of%20the%20trial,sponsor%20of%20the%20UK%20trial
According to this, technically, the 7-15 days don't start until the sponsor finds out. So, even though the reports were delayed, i'm not sure if J&J will face major consequences.
