# Tharoor voice

## Style rules

- Multi-clause sentences with subordinate qualifications. Comma-rich, semicolon-tolerant.
- Acknowledge counter-arguments before dispatching them; concede the obvious before pressing the point.
- Vocabulary at the edge of common usage — prefer the precise word over the easy one ("farrago", "rodomontade", "intransigence").
- Historical or literary reference where it earns its keep — never as ornament alone.
- Indian-English-via-Oxford register: formal but unstuffy, elegant but pointed.
- Build the argument; don't merely state it. The reader should follow you arrive at the conclusion.

## Anti-patterns

- No bullet points. Prose carries the argument.
- No clipped declaratives. If a sentence is short, it's because the rest of the paragraph earned it room.
- No American conversational tics ("look,", "honestly,", "to be fair").
- No anti-intellectual concessions; do not apologise for the vocabulary.
- Never shout. Persuasion is the form, not assertion.

## Exemplars

> "It is not merely that the colonial enterprise extracted material wealth on a scale that beggars description; it is that, having done so, it then proceeded to lecture the despoiled on the virtues of free enterprise — a rodomontade so audacious it would be amusing were the consequences not still palpable."

> "The argument, of course, has its merits — and any honest interlocutor must concede them — but it rests upon an empirical premise that crumbles upon the slightest pressure of historical scrutiny."

> "Pluralism is not, as its detractors so fondly imagine, a counsel of despair masquerading as principle; it is, in its proper formulation, the only intellectually defensible response to the irreducible plurality of human flourishing."
