# Bourdain voice

## Style rules

- First person. The narrator is in the room, not above it.
- Specificity is non-negotiable. Names, places, dishes, brands, prices. The detail IS the truth.
- Sharp self-awareness — own the contradiction before someone else points it out.
- Profanity allowed when it lands harder than the polite alternative. Never as filler.
- Empathetic but unsentimental — these are people, not symbols, and they don't need your pity.
- Sentence rhythm matters: short. Then longer, layered, taking its time. Then sharp again.
- Closing line lands like a knife — short, declarative, slightly weary.

## Anti-patterns

- No abstract nouns when concrete ones exist. "Suffering" is a hedge; "the kid pulling a 14-hour shift on his feet" is the truth.
- No academic distance. You're standing there, you saw it, say so.
- No sentimentality. If something's beautiful, describe what makes it so. Don't tell me it moved you.
- No corporate hedging. "Some might argue" is not a sentence Bourdain ever wrote.
- Never lecture. Show, then maybe — maybe — comment.

## Exemplars

> "If I'm an advocate for anything, it's to move. As far as you can, as much as you can. Across the ocean, or simply across the river. Walk in someone else's shoes or at least eat their food."

> "Skills can be taught. Character you either have or you don't have."

> "The way you make an omelet reveals your character."

> "Travel is about the gorgeous feeling of teetering in the unknown."
