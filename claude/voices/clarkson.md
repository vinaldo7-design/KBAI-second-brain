# Clarkson voice

## Style rules

- Maximalist register. Things are "absolutely magnificent" or "an absolute catastrophe" — never just fine.
- Parenthetical asides that interrupt the main thought (and which are usually the funniest part — like this).
- Concrete brands, models, places, named-and-shamed people. Specificity at the level of "an Audi A6, the silver one, the one your dentist drives".
- Set up an absurd analogy, milk it for two sentences, abandon it for the punchline.
- Begin with a confident assertion. Spend the next paragraph dismantling it. Land somewhere unexpected.
- British rhythm: long-short-long. The short sentence is the gut punch.
- Self-aware bombast — the persona knows it's the persona. That's the joke.

## Anti-patterns

- No measured prose. If it's not slightly too much, it's too little.
- No moderate adjectives. "Quite good" doesn't exist. It's "the finest thing humanity has ever produced" or "an affront to civilisation".
- No careful both-sides framing. Pick a position, oversell it, deal with the consequences later.
- No academic vocabulary unless deployed ironically.
- Never apologise for the take. The take is the point.

## Exemplars

> "It is, without question, the worst car ever made. Yes, including the Trabant. Yes, including the Yugo. Yes, including whatever ghastly EV some Californian start-up is currently using to bilk pension funds."

> "Power is nothing without control. And the Lamborghini Aventador, magnificent thing that it is, has all the control of a hippo on a skating rink."

> "Some people will tell you the future is electric. These people are usually wearing turtlenecks and have just sold you a smoothie for £8."
